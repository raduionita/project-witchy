12 Unusual Period and Cycle Symptoms That Are Common

What are unusual symptoms of your menstrual cycle?

Many cycle symptoms are far from the ones people usually expect. Here are twelve that many people report, grouped roughly by when they tend to appear. Experiences vary, and not everyone gets all of them.

Around the period

  1. Cramps or a dull ache in the lower back. Often linked to prostaglandins released as the lining sheds.
  2. Fatigue and low energy. Common in the days before and during bleeding.
  3. Headaches. Listed by the NHS among common PMS symptoms.
  4. Sore or tender breasts. Common in the luteal phase.

Before the period

  1. Bloating and tummy changes. Hormone shifts can slow digestion for some people.
  2. Breakouts or spotty skin. Listed by the NHS as a possible PMS symptom.
  3. Greasy hair. Some people notice more oil in the days before a period.
  4. Changes in appetite or cravings. Documented in research on PMS and hedonic hunger.

Around ovulation and beyond

  1. Mild pelvic pain. Known as mittelschmerz, often one-sided.
  2. Clear, stretchy discharge. A normal sign of approaching ovulation.
  3. Vivid dreams or sleep changes. Reported by some people, though research is limited.
  4. Mood swings or irritability. Common across the luteal phase.

Easing symptoms

Regular exercise, a balanced diet with smaller meals every few hours, plenty of sleep, stress reduction and simple pain relief can all help, as the NHS suggests. A symptom diary for at least two cycles is useful for spotting your pattern.

When to see a doctor

See a GP if symptoms are severe, affect your daily life, or continue despite lifestyle changes. Bleeding between periods or after sex, very heavy bleeding, or a much longer or shorter cycle than usual should be checked.
