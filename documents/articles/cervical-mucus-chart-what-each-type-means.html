Cervical Mucus Chart: What Each Type Means

What does cervical mucus look like during ovulation?

Cervical mucus is a fluid made by your cervix. Its texture, amount and colour change through your cycle as your hormones shift. Wet, slippery mucus is a sign of fertility, and tracking it can help you spot your most fertile days.

How it changes through the cycle

  • Dry or sticky: Just after your period, mucus is often minimal and dry, or thick and paste-like, and can be white or light yellow. Fertility is low.
  • Creamy: As estrogen rises, mucus can look cloudy or lotion-like. Fertility is building.
  • Egg-white: Near ovulation, mucus is clear, stretchy and slippery, like raw egg white. This is the most fertile type and makes it easier for sperm to reach the egg.
  • Sticky or dry again: After ovulation, progesterone causes mucus to dry up and become thick again, and the fertile window closes.

How to check it

Many people check by wiping with clean fingers or by looking at what is on underwear, then recording what they find each day. Charting for a few cycles usually makes patterns clearer. Your mucus may not look like anyone else's, and that is normal.

What to watch for

If you don't see the slippery, fertile type of mucus at any point, it can sometimes be linked to ovulation problems, infection or other conditions. Check with a doctor if you notice unusual discharge with a strong smell, itching, pain, bleeding between periods, or changes that worry you.
