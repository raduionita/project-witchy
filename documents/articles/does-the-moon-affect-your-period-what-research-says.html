Does the Moon Affect Your Period? What Research Says

Does the moon affect your menstrual cycle?

Probably not in a consistent way. The moon's cycle and the menstrual cycle are both close to a month long, which is why people have linked them for centuries. The research is more mixed than the folklore suggests.

Where the idea comes from

The lunar cycle lasts about 29.5 days, and the average menstrual cycle is about 28 days. That overlap is striking, and many cultures have built stories around it.

What studies have found

A 2021 study in Science Advances followed 22 women who kept menstrual records for up to 32 years. It found that cycles longer than 27 days were sometimes in step with lunar cycles, but this synchrony came and went, and women's cycles varied a lot. Cycles also shortened with age and with exposure to artificial light at night. A larger analysis of about 27,000 cycles from over 3,000 women found only a weak correlation with lunar cycles. A separate large data analysis reported no meaningful correlation. Studies of this kind are still debated, and no consistent lunar effect has been established.

What this means for you

You are very unlikely to start your period on a set moon phase. If you notice a pattern, it may be coincidence, or it may reflect your own body clock and light exposure. Tracking over several months is the best way to find out.

Enjoy the ritual, not the rule

Moon rituals can be a lovely way to mark your cycle. Journaling at the new moon, or noticing how you feel at the full moon, can be a nice habit. Treat it as a personal practice rather than a medical fact.
