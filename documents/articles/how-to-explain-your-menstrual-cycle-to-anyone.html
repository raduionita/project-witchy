How to Explain Your Menstrual Cycle to Anyone

How do you explain the menstrual cycle simply?

You don't need to be a doctor to explain your cycle. A few clear ideas go a long way, whether you're talking to a partner, a colleague, a friend or a child.

The one-sentence version

"Each month, the body gets ready for a possible pregnancy. If there's no pregnancy, the lining is shed, and that's your period."

Simple analogies

  • A monthly build-up: The body prepares a bed in the uterus each month, then clears it away if it isn't needed.
  • Seasons: Each phase has its own feel, like winter rest, spring growth, summer peak and autumn wind-down.
  • A monthly rhythm: Hormones rise and fall, like a tide, and that change can affect energy and mood.

Shareable one-page summary

  1. Menstrual phase: The period, usually three to seven days. Hormones are at their lowest.
  2. Follicular phase: An egg matures in the ovary. Estrogen rises, usually for around 14 to 21 days.
  3. Ovulation: The egg is released, around day 14 of a 28-day cycle.
  4. Luteal phase: Progesterone rises to prepare the lining. It lasts about 12 to 14 days, then the next period starts.

Conversation starters

  • "Is there anything that would help me during my period this week?"
  • "Would you like to know more about how my cycle works?"
  • "Some days I have more energy than others, and that's just my cycle."

Talking to kids

Keep it simple and matter-of-fact. Tell children that periods are a normal, healthy part of growing up, and answer questions honestly, using the correct names for body parts.
