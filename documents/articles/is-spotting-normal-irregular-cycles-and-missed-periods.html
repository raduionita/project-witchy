Is Spotting Normal? Irregular Cycles and Missed Periods

Is it normal to spot between periods?

Sometimes. Spotting between periods is common and often harmless, but it is also worth checking, especially if it keeps happening. Cycles can vary too, and a cycle that doesn't fit the textbook isn't automatically a problem.

What counts as a normal cycle?

The average cycle is 28 days, but a regular cycle can run anywhere from about 21 to 35 days. Periods typically last three to seven days. Cycles can be irregular for the first year or two after your first period, and they can change with stress, weight changes, travel, illness and hormonal contraception.

Common reasons for spotting

  • Ovulation spotting, which is light and brief
  • Starting or changing hormonal contraception, especially in the first few months
  • Stress, significant weight changes or intense exercise
  • Early pregnancy
  • Hormonal changes, including perimenopause
  • Conditions such as polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), which can make periods irregular or light

What to track in your app

Log the start and end date of each period, any spotting and when it happens, flow heaviness, pain, and other symptoms. Patterns over three or more cycles are much more useful than one odd month.

When to see a doctor

Speak to a GP if you have bleeding or spotting between periods or after sex, very heavy bleeding, periods that are much shorter or longer than usual, or a missed period when pregnancy is possible. Spotting after menopause should always be checked.
