Luteal Phase Brain Fog: Causes and What Helps

Why does my brain feel foggy before my period?

The luteal phase runs from ovulation until your next period. It usually lasts about 12 to 14 days. Many people notice tiredness, a dip in focus or a sense of "fog" in the days before a period. This is common, and there are practical ways to ease it.

Why it can happen

After ovulation, the corpus luteum produces progesterone and some estrogen. As these hormones rise and then fall before a period, some people feel sleepy, irritable or less sharp. Premenstrual syndrome (PMS) symptoms, which the NHS describes as occurring in the weeks before a period, can include tiredness, trouble sleeping and mood changes. Sleep disruption can make concentration harder.

Small habits that may help

  • Protect your sleep: aim for a regular bedtime and a wind-down routine. Adults generally need 7 to 9 hours.
  • Eat regular, balanced meals so your energy doesn't crash.
  • Move your body regularly. Gentle exercise is a good option when energy is low.
  • Limit alcohol and cut back on smoking, as both can make PMS symptoms worse.
  • Keep a simple symptom diary for at least two cycles, so you can see your personal pattern.

Normal changes or something more?

Mild, predictable changes that ease once your period starts are common. See a GP if symptoms are affecting your work, relationships or daily life, or if lifestyle changes have not helped. A small number of people have premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD), which causes much more intense symptoms. If you have thoughts of harming yourself, seek help straight away from a doctor or your local emergency number.
