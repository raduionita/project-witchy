Period Goddesses and Menstrual Rituals Around the World

What are some menstrual rituals around the world?

Many cultures have told stories and held rituals around menstruation. Some treated it as sacred, others as dangerous, and many as both. This is folklore and cultural history, not medical fact.

Reverence in older traditions

Historians note that in some ancient contexts menstruation was regarded with respect rather than fear. Stories linked the cycle to fertility, the moon and the rhythm of the seasons. Some traditions celebrated a girl's first period as a turning point into adulthood.

Restriction and separation

In other places, menstruating women were kept apart. Some communities used separate huts or rooms, and some religious traditions limited women's access to sacred places during their periods. These practices were often shaped by fear and beliefs about pollution, and they are now widely questioned.

Coming-of-age and community

Many cultures mark a first period with a ceremony, a gift, or a gathering of family and friends. These celebrations can be warm and affirming, but they also varied in how much they emphasised secrecy or shame.

Making your own gentle ritual

  • Light a candle at the start of your period and take a few quiet minutes to check in with yourself.
  • Keep a cycle journal, and write one thing you're grateful for each phase.
  • Mark the full moon or new moon in a way that feels meaningful to you.
  • Choose a comforting drink or meal to share on the first day of bleeding.
