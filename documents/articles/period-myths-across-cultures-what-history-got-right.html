Period Myths Across Cultures: What History Got Right

What did different cultures believe about periods?

Throughout history, people have explained menstruation in many ways, from sacred to shameful. Many of the old beliefs were wrong, but a few touched on real aspects of the cycle.

Taboo and shame

Menstruating women have often been treated as unclean. Religious texts such as Leviticus describe menstruating women as unclean, and some religious communities have restricted their participation in worship, shared spaces or even their marriages. Some communities still keep menstruating women apart from the rest of the household.

Myths that were wrong

Medical writers in the 19th and early 20th centuries claimed that menstruating women could spoil food, sour milk or cause flowers to wilt. Such claims were repeated for decades and were never supported by good evidence. Modern science has no basis for them.

Beliefs that were closer to the truth

Some older observations were surprisingly accurate. Women in many cultures tracked their cycles by the moon, and the cycle does line up roughly with a lunar month, even if the link is not direct. Some traditions also recognised that periods change with age and life stage, which modern research confirms.

Why the old ideas still matter

Attitudes shaped by these beliefs still influence how periods are talked about, how products are marketed and how girls learn about their bodies. Knowing the history can help people separate inherited shame from medical fact.
