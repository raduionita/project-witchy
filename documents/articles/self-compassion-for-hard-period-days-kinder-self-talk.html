Self-Compassion for Hard Period Days: Kinder Self-Talk

How can I be kinder to myself on hard period days?

Low energy, low mood and irritability are common in the days around a period. You can't always change how you feel, but you can change how you talk to yourself about it.

Notice the harsh voice

Many people say things like "I'm useless" or "I can't cope" on hard days. Notice when this happens, then ask what you would say to a friend in the same situation.

Simple reframes

  • "I'm not lazy. My body needs rest right now."
  • "This feeling is part of my cycle, and it will pass."
  • "I'm doing my best with the energy I have."

Journal prompts

  • What does my body need today, and what can I do to give it that?
  • What is one thing I handled well this week, even though I felt low?
  • What would I say to a friend who felt this way?

Small ways to rest and recover

Gentle movement, a warm bath, a walk outside, an early night, and cutting back on non-essential tasks can all help. Regular sleep, a balanced diet and avoiding too much alcohol support mood and energy. Pain relief can help with cramps, as the NHS notes.

When to reach out

See a GP if low mood lasts beyond your period, if symptoms are affecting your work or relationships, or if lifestyle changes don't help. Some people have premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD), which causes much more severe symptoms and needs medical care.

If you are having thoughts of ending your life or harming yourself, please contact a doctor, a crisis line or your local emergency number straight away. You don't have to get through it alone.
