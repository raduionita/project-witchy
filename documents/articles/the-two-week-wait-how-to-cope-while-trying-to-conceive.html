The Two-Week Wait: How to Cope While Trying to Conceive

What is the two-week wait and how do I cope?

The two-week wait is the time between ovulation, or a possible conception, and when a pregnancy test can give a reliable answer. It usually lasts about 10 to 14 days. For many people it is one of the hardest parts of trying to conceive.

Why early symptoms are unreliable

Many early pregnancy symptoms, such as tender breasts, fatigue, bloating and mood changes, are almost identical to premenstrual symptoms. You can feel symptoms and not be pregnant, and you can be pregnant with no symptoms at all. Searching for signs often increases anxiety without giving a clear answer.

When to test

Home pregnancy tests are highly accurate when used correctly. Some detect pregnancy days before a missed period, but results are most reliable once your period is late. If a test is negative but your period is still late, or you still feel pregnant, test again a few days later. If you get a positive result, contact a healthcare provider.

Gentle ways to cope

  • Set a limit on symptom-checking and searching online.
  • Plan something you enjoy during the wait, even something small.
  • Talk to someone you trust about how you feel.
  • Keep busy with tasks that feel good, and allow rest when you need it.
  • Remember that the wait is temporary, whatever the result.

When to reach out for support

If the waiting is making you very low, anxious or unable to function, speak to a GP or a counsellor. Fertility clinics and support groups can also help.
