What Is the Ovulation Phase? Energy, Mood and Symptoms

What happens during the ovulation phase?

During the ovulation phase, your ovary releases a mature egg. In a 28-day cycle, this usually happens around day 14, but the exact timing varies from person to person and from cycle to cycle.

What happens in the body

A sudden rise in luteinizing hormone (LH) triggers the release of the egg from the follicle. The release itself takes only a few seconds. The egg can survive for about 12 to 24 hours afterwards. Ovulation marks the end of the follicular phase and the start of the luteal phase.

Common signs people notice

  • Clear, stretchy, egg-white cervical mucus
  • A slight rise in basal body temperature, typically about 0.5 to 1 degree
  • Mild pelvic or lower abdominal pain, sometimes on one side (called mittelschmerz)
  • Sore breasts, bloating or a change in sex drive

Mood, energy and focus

Many people report feeling more energetic, sociable or focused around ovulation, and some notice mood changes. Rising estrogen is one possible reason. These changes vary a lot, and research on them is limited, so they are best treated as personal patterns to track rather than a rule.

Why plenty of people feel nothing special

Lots of people don't notice any clear symptoms at all. That is normal. Ovulation can happen without any signs, and the absence of symptoms doesn't mean something is wrong.

Ovulation and your fertile window

Sex in the five days before ovulation, or on the day of ovulation, gives the highest chance of pregnancy, because sperm can survive in the body for several days. The egg itself is only viable for about a day. Ovulation predictor kits detect LH in urine, usually starting around day 10 of the cycle, and a positive result usually means ovulation within about 36 hours.

When to see a doctor

If your cycles are very irregular, you have no periods, or you are trying to conceive without success, speak to a doctor. Severe or unusual pain should also be checked.
