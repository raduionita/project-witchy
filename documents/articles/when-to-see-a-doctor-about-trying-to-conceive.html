When to See a Doctor About Trying to Conceive

When should I see a doctor about trying to get pregnant?

Many doctors suggest seeking help after 12 months of trying to conceive, or after six months if you are 35 or older. You can ask for help sooner if you have concerns, and it is always reasonable to do so.

What infertility means

The American Society for Reproductive Medicine (ASRM) describes infertility as the inability to conceive after 12 months of unprotected intercourse. Its updated definition also takes into account a person's medical, sexual and reproductive history, age and physical findings, so the right point to seek help can vary.

Reasons to ask for help earlier

  • You are over 35, or have a known condition that affects fertility.
  • Your cycles are very irregular, very long, very short, or absent.
  • You have had several miscarriages.
  • You have had treatment that may affect fertility, or surgery on the reproductive organs.

What a first visit involves

A first appointment usually includes a discussion of your medical history, your cycle and your lifestyle. Your doctor may suggest tests for ovulation, hormone levels, the health of the uterus and fallopian tubes, and sometimes a partner's semen analysis. Bringing your cycle tracking records can save time.

Why asking early is fine

Getting an assessment doesn't mean something is wrong. It gives you information and options. Many people find that early evaluation brings reassurance, or identifies something treatable.
